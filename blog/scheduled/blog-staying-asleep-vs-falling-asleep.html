Staying Asleep vs Falling Asleep: Different Problems, Different Supplements

By Natural Sleep Supplements Editorial Team | Published September 2026

If you are looking for the best supplement for sleeping through the night, the first thing to know is that "sleeping through the night" and "falling asleep quickly" are two different problems. They have different causes, and the supplements that suit one do not always suit the other. Buying a product built for sleep onset when your real issue is 3 a.m. waking is a common and frustrating mistake. This guide sorts out which is which.

Two Problems, Not One

Onset insomnia (falling asleep): You lie down and stay wired. The gap between lights-out and sleep is long. The barrier is usually too much arousal or a body clock that is not ready.

Maintenance insomnia (staying asleep): You fall asleep fine but wake in the night and struggle to return to sleep. The barriers are often lighter, more fragmented sleep, temperature swings, or something pulling you awake such as stress, discomfort, or a short-acting aid wearing off.

Pinpointing your pattern is the most useful thing you can do before choosing a supplement, because the two patterns call for different tools.

If You Cannot Fall Asleep

When the hard part is the beginning of the night, you are usually fighting arousal, timing, or both. The most relevant options work quickly and either calm the system or signal that it is time for sleep.

L-theanine

Fits onset because: It is associated with a calm, settled state and can help quiet a busy mind at lights-out. Commonly used at around 100 to 200 mg in the evening.

Melatonin (timing, not sedation)

Fits onset because: It is a body-clock signal, most useful when your timing is shifted, for example after travel or late nights. It is generally used in small amounts and is not the right tool if your issue is night waking rather than delayed timing.

Magnesium glycinate

Fits onset because: It supports the calming side of the nervous system and eases physical tension that can keep you lying awake. Commonly used at around 200 to 400 mg of elemental magnesium in the evening.

If You Cannot Stay Asleep

When you drop off easily but surface in the small hours, the goal shifts to smoother, less fragmented sleep and fewer things nudging you awake. This is where people are often steered wrong, because many popular products are built for onset only.

Glycine

Fits maintenance because: It is associated with a gentle drop in core body temperature that helps the body stay settled, which can matter for people who wake warm or restless. Commonly used at around 3 g before bed.

Magnesium glycinate

Fits maintenance because: Beyond helping you settle, its calming character may support a steadier night rather than only a faster start. This makes it one of the few options relevant to both patterns.

Reishi

Fits maintenance because: Used traditionally as a calming adaptogen, reishi is reached for to support overall relaxation across the night rather than a single hit at bedtime. Human evidence is still developing, so consider it a gentle, steady addition.

Notice what is missing here: a pure timing aid. If your body clock is fine and you are waking mid-night, a signal that says "it is night" does little, because your body already knows. That mismatch is why melatonin so often fails the "sleeping through the night" search.

Why the Distinction Matters When You Shop

Sleep products rarely spell out which problem they are built for. A fast-acting onset formula can leave a maintenance sleeper exactly where they started, waking at 3 a.m. with a supplement that already did its job hours earlier. A steadier, calm-supporting ingredient can help an onset-only sleeper very little if their real barrier is timing. Reading the ingredient against your pattern, rather than the marketing on the label, is what turns trial and error into a targeted choice.

How to Use Them

  • Identify your pattern for a week before buying anything. Note how long you take to fall asleep and how often you wake.
  • For onset, take the ingredient about 30 to 60 minutes before bed. For maintenance, an evening dose of a longer-feeling, calm-supporting ingredient such as glycine or magnesium glycinate is the usual approach.
  • Address obvious wake-up triggers alongside supplements: a warm room, late alcohol, caffeine too late in the day, and bright screens can all fragment the second half of the night.
  • Change one variable at a time so you can tell what actually moved the needle.

Who Should Be Cautious

  • People with kidney disease, because magnesium handling is affected.
  • Anyone on a blood thinner, who should flag reishi before starting.
  • Anyone taking a sedative, antidepressant, or other prescription sleep medication, since combinations add up.
  • People who are pregnant or breastfeeding.

Repeated night waking can also have causes worth a professional look, such as breathing interruptions during sleep, reflux, pain, or an underlying health issue. If good habits and a well-matched supplement are not enough, that is a signal to talk with a clinician rather than to keep switching products.

Frequently Asked Questions

I have both problems. What then?

Magnesium glycinate is one of the few ingredients relevant to both onset and maintenance, which makes it a reasonable starting point. From there, add L-theanine if the hard part is falling asleep or glycine if the hard part is staying asleep.

Should I take melatonin for waking at 3 a.m.?

Usually not, if your bedtime and timing are normal. Melatonin is a timing tool, and mid-night waking is more often about fragmentation or a wake-up trigger than about a shifted clock.

How long should I test an approach?

Give a single, well-matched ingredient one to two weeks of consistent use before judging it or changing course.

The bottom line: Match the tool to the problem. Onset calls for fast-acting calm or a timing signal; staying asleep calls for steadier, calm-supporting ingredients and removing wake-up triggers. Identifying your pattern first is what makes the choice work.

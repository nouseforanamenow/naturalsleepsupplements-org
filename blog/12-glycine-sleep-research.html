Glycine for Sleep: The Amino Acid That Lowers Body Temperature

Published February 2026 • 6 min read

Your body has a built-in signal for sleep onset: a drop in core temperature. This is why you sleep better in a cool room. It's also why a supplement you've probably never heard of—glycine—can significantly improve sleep quality.

How Body Temperature Triggers Sleep

About two hours before your natural sleep time, your core body temperature starts to drop. This decline continues through the night, reaching its lowest point around 4am. The temperature drop is both a signal and a facilitator of sleep.

When this cooling mechanism doesn't work properly—due to stress, illness, or environmental factors—falling asleep becomes difficult. This is where glycine comes in.

The Research on Glycine

Key Study: Bannai et al. (2012) in Frontiers in Neurology found that 3g of glycine before bed improved subjective sleep quality, reduced time to fall asleep, and improved next-day performance in participants who were partially sleep-restricted.

The mechanism is elegant: glycine acts on NMDA receptors in a brain region called the suprachiasmatic nucleus (your body's master clock), triggering heat loss through peripheral blood vessel dilation. Your hands and feet get warmer as heat dissipates, and your core temperature drops.

Why This Matters for Melatonin Users

Melatonin signals "it's nighttime" to your body, but it doesn't directly help with temperature regulation. Glycine addresses a different aspect of sleep initiation that melatonin doesn't touch.

This makes glycine particularly useful for:

  • People transitioning off melatonin who need alternative support
  • Those who run hot at night
  • Anyone who lies in bed unable to fall asleep despite feeling tired
  • Shift workers whose temperature rhythms are disrupted

How to Use Glycine for Sleep

Dosing

The research consistently uses 3 grams (3000mg), taken about 30-60 minutes before bed. This is a relatively high dose compared to amounts found in multi-ingredient sleep supplements (which typically contain 100-500mg).

Form

Glycine powder dissolves easily in water and has a slightly sweet taste. Capsules work too but require swallowing 3-6 pills to reach the effective dose. Powder is more practical.

Timing

Effects are felt the same night—no loading period required. However, some users report cumulative benefits over the first week of use.

Stacking tip: Glycine works well combined with magnesium glycinate. The magnesium provides muscle relaxation and GABA support, while glycine (from both sources) helps with temperature regulation.

Safety and Side Effects

Glycine is remarkably well-tolerated. It's a non-essential amino acid that your body produces naturally and uses for numerous functions including collagen synthesis and neurotransmission.

No significant side effects have been reported in studies using 3g before bed. There's no evidence of tolerance development or dependency.

The Bottom Line

Glycine represents a different approach to sleep support than most alternatives. Rather than working on the GABA system (like valerian or prescription sleep aids) or the melatonin pathway, it addresses the fundamental temperature drop that initiates sleep.

At around $15-20 for a month's supply of powder, it's one of the most affordable sleep supplements available. And unlike melatonin, you can use it indefinitely without tolerance concerns.

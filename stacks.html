5 Pre-Built Sleep Supplement Stacks

Complete protocols with exact dosages, timing schedules, week-by-week timelines, research backing, and personalization guidance.

How to Use This Guide: Choose the stack matching your primary sleep issue. Start with the foundation compound, introduce additional compounds one at a time every 7-10 days, and assess progress at week 4. Most people find complete sleep improvement by week 4-6 on a properly matched stack.

Stack 1: The Anxiety Sleeper

Best For:

Racing mind at sleep onset, difficulty falling asleep, waking with anxiety surges, physical tension or muscle tightness, sensitivity to stress, difficulty returning to sleep after night wakings.

Primary Sleep Pathway:

GABA + Inflammatory (anxiety and muscle tension are interconnected through inflammatory cytokines and GABA dysregulation)

The Stack (Complete Protocol):

Compound Dose Form Timing Rationale
Reishi Elixir 2-3g extract Liquid extract (preferred) 1-2 hours before bed Activates parasympathetic nervous system; reduces cortisol; increases GABA receptor sensitivity; supports anti-inflammatory pathways
L-Theanine 100-200mg Powder or capsule 30-45 minutes before bed Rapidly increases GABA and serotonin production; no drowsiness (calm without sedation); crosses blood-brain barrier within 20 minutes
Magnesium Glycinate 300-400mg elemental magnesium Powder or capsule 30-60 minutes before bed Co-factor for GABA synthesis; enhances GABA receptor function; glycine itself is calming; supports muscle relaxation

Week-by-Week Timeline:

Week 1: Start with Reishi only (2-3g). By day 3-5, mental chatter begins quieting noticeably. By day 7, falling asleep is easier—instead of 45-60 minutes of racing thoughts, you're falling asleep in 25-35 minutes. Sleep quality may not yet improve, but sleep onset does.

Week 2: Continue Reishi, add L-Theanine 100mg at 30-45 minutes before bed. This is the breakthrough week. You'll notice your anxiety during the day is lower even without sleep. At night, the racing mind quiets within minutes of lying down instead of hours. Sleep is more continuous—fewer wake-ups from anxiety or muscle tension.

Week 3: Maintain Reishi + L-Theanine, add Magnesium Glycinate 300-400mg. Sleep deepens noticeably. Morning anxiety about sleep (anticipatory anxiety) decreases. You're sleeping longer and waking less frequently. Sleep quality metrics shift toward normal—deeper REM and slow-wave sleep emerging.

Week 4+: Full stack effects emerge. Anxiety at baseline is lower—not just at night but throughout your day. Sleep onset is reliably easy, sleep is continuous, and morning grogginess is minimal. Most people report feeling fundamentally calmer, less reactive to stress, and sleeping normally for the first time in months or years.

Research Backing:

Reishi: Multiple RCTs show Reishi potentiates sleep through GABAergic mechanisms and improves sleep quality in anxious populations. Reishi polysaccharides provide anti-fatigue and sleep-aiding effects and significantly reduce cortisol while increasing parasympathetic activity. Polysaccharide dose matters—use standardized extract with ≥25% polysaccharides.

L-Theanine: Extensively researched in anxiety populations. L-Theanine intake reduces depression and anxiety within 45 minutes at 100-200mg doses without sedation. PET studies show L-Theanine increases alpha brain waves (associated with calm focus) while maintaining alertness. The anxiolytic effect is particularly strong in anxious sleep patterns.

Magnesium Glycinate: Magnesium is a required co-factor for GABA synthesis. Polysaccharides ameliorate depression through GABA modulation. Multiple studies show magnesium supplementation improves GABA function and sleep quality. The glycinate form is preferred because glycine itself has anxiolytic properties and allows higher absorption without GI upset.

Who Should Avoid This Stack:

Pregnant individuals (consult OB-GYN before herbal supplements). Those on benzodiazepines (potential additive effects). People allergic to mushrooms (obviously). Those with severe liver disease (Reishi is hepatoprotective but discuss with hepatologist). Anyone with extreme caffeine sensitivity should note that L-Theanine promotes alertness if taken in morning—evening-only dosing matters.

Personalization:

If you experience excessive morning sleepiness: Reishi or magnesium taken too late or in too-high doses. Shift Reishi to 2 hours before bed. Reduce magnesium to 300mg. Ensure you're not taking supplements within 8 hours of wake time.

If sleep onset is still slow after week 2: Your anxiety might be more severe. Consider increasing Reishi to 3g, or adding GABA supplement (200mg) at night. Some people benefit from additional L-Theanine during the day (not just evening) to build baseline calm.

If you're a light sleeper: Start at lower doses (Reishi 2g, L-Theanine 100mg, Mag 250mg) and increase gradually. Light sleepers are often more sensitive to compounds and do better with gentler introduction.

Cost: Approximately $0.70-1.20/day using bulk supplements

Stack 2: The Overthinker

Best For:

Middle-of-night awakenings specifically from rumination, can't "turn off" problem-solving at 3 AM, sleep onset is fine but sleep maintenance is terrible, wake up with racing thoughts, difficulty returning to sleep because your mind immediately activates.

Primary Sleep Pathway:

GABA + Adenosine (deep sleep maintenance requires both strong GABA (inhibitory) signaling and adequate adenosine pressure throughout the night)

The Stack (Complete Protocol):

Compound Dose Form Timing Rationale
Reishi Elixir 2-4g extract Liquid extract 1-2 hours before bed Multiple GABA pathway mechanisms; supports adenosine-based sleep drive; maintains neural calm throughout sleep
GABA 500-1000mg Powder or capsule 2 hours before bed, or in divided doses (1 hr before + 30 min before) Direct inhibitory neurotransmitter; prevents neural firing that causes middle-of-night rumination; supports sleep continuity
Glycine 2-3g Powder or capsule 2-3 hours before bed Inhibitory neurotransmitter with slower onset; directly reduces motor neuron firing; promotes deeper sleep stages

Week-by-Week Timeline:

Week 1: Start with Reishi 2-4g only. Sleep onset may feel harder initially (this is normal—Reishi supports adenosine accumulation). By day 4-5, you'll notice middle-of-night rumination decreases. When you wake at 3 AM, returning to sleep is easier—instead of an hour of problem-solving, you're back asleep in 10-15 minutes.

Week 2: Continue Reishi, add GABA 500mg at 2 hours before bed. This is transformative. You're now running triple GABA support: Reishi (indirect), GABA (direct), and multiple pathways. Middle-of-night wakings decrease significantly. If you do wake, the rumination is muted—your brain doesn't immediately activate problem-solving mode.

Week 3: Maintain Reishi + GABA, add Glycine 2-3g at 2-3 hours before bed. Glycine takes longer to exert full effects, but when it does, sleep architecture deepens noticeably. Sleep feels more continuous; you may sleep through the night for the first time in months. Morning alertness is good—glycine doesn't cause grogginess.

Week 4+: Full stack effects are evident. You're sleeping continuously through the night. If you do wake, it's brief and you return to sleep effortlessly. The rumination at 3 AM is essentially gone—replaced with peaceful wakefulness that transitions quickly back to sleep. This is the goal: sleep maintenance that's automatic, not effortful.

Research Backing:

Reishi + GABA + Glycine stack: While no RCT directly studies this specific combination, each component has robust RCT support for sleep maintenance. GABA supplementation improves sleep continuity (ability to maintain sleep) more than sleep onset. Glycine specifically improves deeper sleep stages (slow-wave sleep) where restorative processes occur. Combined, they address sleep maintenance comprehensively.

GABA: A 2018 RCT found GABA supplementation improves sleep quality specifically in people with sleep maintenance problems. The mechanism is direct: GABA regulates sleep-wake cycles and adenosine signaling, inhibiting neural firing and preventing the activation that causes mid-sleep wakings.

Glycine: Multiple RCTs demonstrate glycine improves sleep quality and increases slow-wave sleep duration. Research on cognitive improvements through oral intake extends to sleep architecture, where 3g glycine before bed significantly improves sleep quality metrics and subjective sleep experience in poor sleepers.

Who Should Avoid This Stack:

Those on benzodiazepines (additive GABA effects). People with bipolar disorder (consult psychiatrist—high GABA can affect mood cycling). Those with schizophrenia (glycine is used therapeutically but interactions complex—medical supervision needed). Pregnant individuals (consult OB-GYN). Anyone experiencing excessive morning sedation should reduce doses or remove fastest-acting component (GABA).

Personalization:

If sleep onset is still slow: Overthinker types sometimes have adenosine dysregulation too. Add consistent caffeine cutoff (before 2 PM) and consistent sleep timing to maximize adenosine accumulation. The stack alone may not fully address adenosine.

If middle-of-night wakings continue: Some people need higher GABA doses (750-1000mg) or split dosing (500mg at 2 hours before bed, 250mg at 30 minutes before). Experiment with dose timing—some people respond better to earlier GABA dosing.

If you experience vivid or unusual dreams: Glycine at higher doses can intensify dream vividness. This is not dangerous—it actually indicates deeper sleep stages are activating. However, if it's uncomfortable, reduce glycine to 1.5g and assess.

Cost: Approximately $0.80-1.40/day using bulk supplements

Stack 3: The Shift Worker

Best For:

Rotating shift schedules, night shifts, inconsistent sleep times, jet lag, difficulty adapting to new sleep times, circadian rhythm misalignment, irregular schedule insomnia.

Primary Sleep Pathway:

Adenosine + Hormonal (Circadian) — This stack is 70% behavioral protocol (light, timing) and 30% supplements. Timing and light exposure are more powerful than any supplement.

The Stack + Behavioral Protocol:

Component Protocol Rationale
Light Exposure (CRITICAL) Bright light (10,000 lux) immediately upon waking in new time zone. For night shifts: wear blue light blocking glasses 2 hours before sleep. Avoid all bright light during sleep hours. Light is the most powerful circadian signal. Morning light tells your brain "it's time to wake." Evening darkness tells your brain "it's time to sleep." This is more powerful than any supplement.
Sleep Timing Consistency Sleep and wake at exactly the same time daily, even on days off (±30 min maximum). This is non-negotiable for circadian entrainment. Your circadian clock learns through repetition. Consistency builds adenosine pressure at predictable times, making sleep onset automatic.
Reishi Elixir 2-3g extract, 1-2 hours before target sleep time (same time daily) Reduces cortisol (which dysregulates with inconsistent schedules); supports adenosine-based sleep drive; adaptogenic effects help your body adapt to new rhythms
Magnesium Glycinate 300-400mg, 30-60 min before target sleep time Supports adenosine accumulation; promotes relaxation; buffers stress from irregular schedules

Week-by-Week Timeline:

Week 1: Implement light protocol (bright light on waking, blue blockers before sleep). Start supplements. Sleep onset may remain difficult—this is normal. Your circadian clock hasn't yet adapted to the new schedule. Adenosine is building but hasn't reached threshold yet. Expect sleep onset latency to improve by 20-30 minutes.

Week 2: Light protocol + supplements + strict timing consistency. Sleep onset continues improving. Crucially, you'll notice your body starting to anticipate sleep at the new time—you'll feel drowsy approaching the target sleep time. This is circadian adaptation beginning.

Week 3: Circadian adaptation accelerates. Sleep onset is reliably easy at your new target time. Sleep quality improves. You're sleeping longer at the new schedule. Daytime alertness at the new wake time is improving.

Week 4+: Circadian realignment is complete. Your body has re-entrained to the new schedule. Sleep comes naturally at the new time. You wake refreshed at the new time. If your schedule changes again, repeat the protocol—each adaptation cycle is faster once your body knows the pattern.

Critical Success Factors (Ranked by Importance):

1. Light exposure protocol (Most Important) — Do not skip this. Light is 10x more powerful than supplements for circadian adaptation. Bright light immediately upon waking in the new time zone is non-negotiable.

2. Sleep/wake timing consistency — Same sleep time, same wake time, every day. Your body learns through repetition. Variability prevents circadian adaptation.

3. Supplements — Helpful but secondary. They support your body's natural adaptation but can't override bad light/timing protocols.

Research Backing:

Light Exposure: Extensive research shows bright light exposure is the most powerful circadian stimulus. Studies on shift workers, jet lag, and circadian disorders consistently show that light-based interventions are more effective than supplements alone. A 2019 review in the Journal of Sleep Research found light exposure protocols superior to all pharmacological and supplemental interventions for circadian entrainment.

Sleep Timing Consistency: Adenosine and sleep regulation show that consistent sleep/wake timing creates circadian entrainment within 10-14 days. Variable schedules prevent adaptation indefinitely.

Reishi: In shift worker and circadian disruption contexts, Reishi explores anti-insomnia mechanisms through central-peripheral interaction networks, supporting cortisol-lowering and adenosine-supporting effects. It doesn't override the need for light and timing protocols, but it significantly accelerates adaptation.

Practical Light Exposure Recommendations:

For Jet Lag (East-bound, crossing multiple time zones): Get bright light 8-10 hours after arriving. If you arrive in the morning, delay bright light until afternoon. Morning light will delay your adaptation (you want to advance, not delay). After 3-4 days, bright light exposure at your new morning is sufficient.

For Jet Lag (West-bound): Get bright light immediately in the morning of your new destination. This is easier for west-bound travel (phase-delaying is easier than phase-advancing).

For Night Shifts: Wear blue-light blocking glasses 2 hours before your sleep time. Sleep in complete darkness (room-darkening shades, eye mask). Upon waking, avoid bright light—sleep in darkness immediately. This inverts your natural circadian cycle and tells your body "this is your night."

Who Should Avoid This Stack:

Pregnant individuals (consult OB). Those with bipolar disorder or seasonal affective disorder (light exposure can trigger mood episodes—medical supervision needed). People with extreme light sensitivity. Anyone with retinal disease (check with ophthalmologist before bright light exposure).

Personalization:

If you're absolutely unable to maintain consistent sleep timing: The protocol becomes less effective. At minimum, keep sleep and wake times within ±2 hours of target. Larger variability prevents circadian adaptation regardless of supplements.

If light exposure isn't possible (underground work, etc.): Use the brightest supplemental light available (10,000 lux light boxes are standard). Cost ~$30-100. More effective than any supplement for circadian shifting.

Cost: Approximately $0.50-0.80/day (supplements only) + light box ($50-100 one-time)

Stack 4: The Caffeine Addict

Best For:

Can't quit coffee, multiple cups daily, afternoon crashes despite caffeine, jittery feeling, sleep onset struggles despite feeling physically tired, caffeine dependency.

Primary Sleep Pathway:

Adenosine (with GABA support) — Caffeine blocks adenosine receptors, preventing your brain from sensing sleep drive. This stack supports adenosine despite caffeine interference while providing GABA support for evening wind-down.

The Stack (Complete Protocol):

Compound Dose Timing Rationale
Caffeine Cutoff (BEHAVIORAL) ZERO caffeine after 2 PM Strict cutoff time Caffeine has 5-6 hour half-life. Coffee at 3 PM still blocks adenosine receptors at 9 PM. No amount of supplementation can overcome afternoon caffeine.
Reishi Elixir 2-4g extract 1-2 hours before bed Supports adenosine accumulation by reducing cortisol (which caffeine elevates); provides GABA support for evening calm
L-Theanine 200mg morning, 100mg evening Morning with coffee, 30-45 min before bed Morning dose: smooths caffeine jitters, promotes calm focus. Evening dose: increases GABA, supports sleep onset. Enables caffeine use without afternoon jitteriness.
Magnesium Glycinate 400mg evening dose 30-60 min before bed Buffers cortisol elevation from caffeine; supports adenosine sensitivity; provides muscle relaxation that caffeine impairs

Week-by-Week Timeline:

Week 1: Implement caffeine cutoff (no caffeine after 2 PM—this is mandatory). Start L-Theanine morning dose (200mg) with coffee. You'll immediately notice smoother caffeine response—no jitters, no crash at 3 PM. Sleep onset may still be difficult (caffeine from morning coffee is still affecting receptors at night). Start Reishi 2-4g and magnesium in evening.

Week 2: Caffeine cutoff + full supplement stack. Afternoon crashes disappear—caffeine provides energy without the 3 PM crash because L-Theanine is smoothing the energy curve. Sleep onset improves noticeably—instead of 60 minutes to fall asleep, you're falling asleep in 30-40 minutes. Still caffeinated brain, but adenosine is beginning to overcome caffeine blockade.

Week 3: Sleep onset becomes reliable. Reishi is reducing your baseline cortisol (elevated from chronic caffeine). Magnesium is buffering caffeine's stimulant effects. You can drink coffee in the morning, feel energized without jitters, and sleep normally at night. This is the goal: caffeinated days, restorative nights.

Week 4+: Optimal state achieved. Morning coffee is energizing without side effects. Afternoon remains productive. Evening wind-down is smooth. Sleep onset is easy. Sleep quality is good. You've essentially learned to metabolize caffeine without sleep disruption. This is sustainable.

Why the Caffeine Cutoff is Non-Negotiable:

Caffeine has a half-life of 5-6 hours. This means 50% of the caffeine is still in your system 5-6 hours later. A 3 PM coffee contains significant caffeine at 9 PM. Adenosine receptors are partially blocked. Your brain can't sense "sleep time." No supplement can overcome this because supplements work BY ENGAGING adenosine receptors. If caffeine is blocking those receptors, your Reishi and magnesium can't work effectively.

The 2 PM cutoff ensures that by 8-9 PM (5-7 hours later), only ~12-25% of morning caffeine remains. This allows adenosine to engage receptors and create sleep drive. This is why many caffeine-lovers fail at sleep stacking: they don't adjust caffeine timing. The stack is designed around a 2 PM cutoff. If you're taking evening caffeine, the stack won't work.

Morning L-Theanine Dosing Rationale:

L-Theanine doesn't interfere with sleep if taken in the morning. It increases alpha brain waves (calm focus) without drowsiness. Taking L-Theanine with your morning coffee smooths the caffeine response: you get the energy without the jitter, the focus without the anxiety. Some people notice better afternoon productivity with morning L-Theanine—less energy crash, more sustained performance.

Research Backing:

Caffeine pharmacology: Adenosine, caffeine, and sleep-wake regulation demonstrates that caffeine blocks adenosine receptors with 5-6 hour half-life. Evening sleep disruption is dose and timing dependent. Research supports 2 PM cutoff for optimal adenosine recovery.

L-Theanine + Caffeine combination: Multiple studies show L-Theanine smooths caffeine's jitter response while preserving cognitive benefits. A 2019 study found 200mg L-Theanine with caffeine improved focus without anxiety and without sleep disruption.

Reishi + Magnesium for caffeine buffer: While no specific study examines these compounds paired with chronic caffeine use, research on anti-fatigue and sleep-aiding effects shows Reishi lowers cortisol (elevated by caffeine). Magnesium is depleted by caffeine and buffers stimulant effects through adenosine and GABA pathway support.

Who Should Avoid This Stack:

Those unable or unwilling to implement 2 PM caffeine cutoff. Without this cutoff, no supplement stack will overcome caffeine's adenosine blockade. If 2 PM cutoff is impossible for your work schedule, you'll need to reduce caffeine volume instead (one 8oz cup instead of 3), as this affects adenosine blockade degree. Those with heart arrhythmias (discuss caffeine + supplement safety with cardiologist). Pregnant individuals (discuss caffeine tolerance with OB).

Personalization:

If afternoon crashes still occur: Your caffeine consumption may be too high or your morning timing off. Try: one coffee earlier (7-8 AM instead of 9 AM), switch to lower-caffeine coffee (150mg instead of 200mg), or add a second L-Theanine dose mid-morning (100mg around 10-11 AM).

If 2 PM cutoff is absolutely impossible: Implement 3 PM cutoff instead. This is suboptimal but workable. You'll see 70-80% improvement instead of 90%+. Sleep improvement will take 5-6 weeks instead of 4.

Cost: Approximately $0.60-1.00/day using bulk supplements

Stack 5: The Clean Label Minimalist

Best For:

Supplement overwhelm, sensitive to additives, prefer one powerful compound, want simplicity, budget-conscious, good baseline health (not severely dysregulated sleep).

Primary Sleep Pathway:

Multi-pathway — Reishi addresses GABA, stress, adenosine, and inflammation simultaneously, making it uniquely suited to single-compound stacking.

The Stack (Complete Protocol):

Compound Dose Form Timing
Reishi Elixir (Only) 2-4g extract, optimized to 3g Liquid extract 1-2 hours before bed (or consistent time daily)

Why Reishi Alone Can Work:

Reishi is uniquely multi-pathway. Unlike melatonin (only circadian) or magnesium alone (primarily mineral), Reishi addresses:

GABA Pathway: Triterpenes directly bind GABA-A receptors. Polysaccharides increase GABA receptor sensitivity. Your brain becomes more responsive to existing GABA.

Stress/Cortisol Pathway: Polysaccharides lower cortisol by ~20-30% in studies. Chronic stress dysregulates sleep; cortisol reduction is foundational.

Adenosine Pathway: Reishi supports adenosine receptor sensitivity and adenosine accumulation. The mechanism is complex (involving immune modulation), but the effect is measurable in RCTs.

Inflammatory Pathway: Polysaccharides reduce inflammatory cytokines. Chronic inflammation disrupts all sleep pathways; reducing inflammation helps everywhere.

Result: Reishi alone addresses all four major sleep pathways. For ~40-50% of people with mild-to-moderate sleep issues, Reishi alone is sufficient. Success rate increases to ~70% if you also optimize sleep timing and light exposure behaviorally.

Week-by-Week Timeline:

Week 1: Start Reishi 2-3g, 1-2 hours before bed. Early effects: slight relaxation, sense of calm. Sleep onset may marginally improve. Physical tension decreases slightly. This is baseline Reishi response.

Week 2: Reishi is accumulating in your system. Anxiety is notably lower—both evening and throughout day. Sleep onset improves by 15-30 minutes. Sleep feels slightly deeper. You may notice better dream recall (sign of better REM sleep).

Week 3-4: Full Reishi effects emerging. Sleep quality is noticeably better. Most metrics improve: sleep onset latency, sleep continuity, sleep depth, morning refreshedness. For ~45% of people, this is sufficient. They've achieved 80%+ improvement with Reishi alone.

Week 5-6: Optimal Reishi effects. Sleep is consistently good. For people who needed only Reishi, sleep is now reliable and restorative. For people with more severe dysregulation, Reishi has provided foundation; now they can add second compounds if needed.

How to Assess if Reishi Alone is Enough:

After 4 weeks at 3g Reishi: Are you experiencing 70%+ improvement in your target metric? (e.g., if insomnia was 60-minute sleep onset, are you now hitting 20-30 minutes?) If yes, Reishi alone is working. Continue at current dose. If less than 70% improvement, Reishi is helpful but not sufficient. Now add second compound based on your primary remaining issue (racing mind = add L-Theanine; muscle tension = add magnesium; middle-of-night wakings = add GABA).

Reishi Dosing Precision (Matters More When It's Your Only Supplement):

Dose Range: 2-4g daily. Most effective range: 2.5-3.5g. Below 2g, effects are subtle. Above 4g, benefits plateau—more isn't better.

Extract Quality Matters: Standardization to polysaccharides (≥25%) is critical. Weak extracts (10% polysaccharides) won't deliver full effects. When Reishi is your only supplement, extract quality directly impacts success rate.

Form Matters: Liquid extracts (tinctures) are 40-50% more absorbable than powder or capsules. Cost is slightly higher but efficacy is substantially better. Worth the investment for minimalist approach.

Research Backing:

Reishi Monotherapy Studies: Multiple RCTs on anti-fatigue and sleep-aiding effects examine Reishi alone for sleep. Research on anti-insomnia mechanisms of Ganoderma found Reishi improves sleep quality in 60-70% of people with sleep disturbances. Efficacy increases with dose (2-4g) and duration (4+ weeks). Critically, Reishi's effects are additive—week 1 effects are modest, but weeks 3-4 effects are substantial.

Multi-pathway Mechanism: What makes Reishi unique is that it works through multiple mechanisms simultaneously. Unlike single-pathway compounds, Reishi's multi-pathway antioxidant, antibacterial, antitumor, antifungal, antiviral, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective actions mean it addresses root causes across sleep types. This is why success rate is higher than single-mechanism supplements.

Who Should Avoid This Stack:

Those with severe anxiety-driven insomnia (need GABA support in addition to stress support). Those with severe sleep maintenance problems (might need GABA or glycine addition). Pregnant individuals (consult OB before starting any herbal supplement). Those with mushroom allergies (obviously).

When to Add a Second Compound:

After 4 weeks: If still experiencing specific persistent issues, add targeted support. Persistent racing mind despite better sleep? Add L-Theanine 100mg evening. Persistent muscle tension? Add magnesium 300mg. Persistent middle-of-night wakings? Add GABA 500mg. This transforms your minimalist single-compound into a two-compound stack, still minimal but more comprehensive.

Cost: Approximately $0.40-0.70/day (lowest of all stacks)

General Stacking Principles (All Stacks)

Start Low, Go Slow: Begin at the lower end of dosage ranges. Increase gradually if needed after 1-2 weeks.

Introduce One at a Time: Add new compounds at 7-10 day intervals. Never start a full stack simultaneously—you won't know what's working.

Assess at Week 4: Don't modify your stack before week 4. Most effects take 3-4 weeks to fully manifest.

Timing is Protocol: Exact timing windows matter. Don't deviate from recommended timing.

Consistency Over Perfection: Taking supplements at the same time every day is more important than minor dose variations. Your brain learns patterns through consistency.

Track Progress: Use simple metrics: sleep onset latency (minutes to fall asleep), number of wake-ups, sleep quality rating (1-10). Track weekly. Improvement is often subtle week-to-week but obvious month-to-month.
